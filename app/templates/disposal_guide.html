Medicine & Sharps Disposal Guide

Where to get rid of medicines, needles, and other items you can't use anymore. Dane County, Wisconsin resources are listed first.

⚠️

Never put needles or syringes in a medicine drop box.

They can hurt workers and damage the collection box. Use a sharps container instead.

Have medicine that's still sealed and not expired? It may be donatable instead — see the Drug Repository Site Finder.

💊 Medicines you no longer need

Don't flush medicines or pour them down the drain — it can harm the environment and contaminate lakes and rivers.

MedDrop boxes (Dane County)

MedDrop boxes are the easiest way to get rid of unwanted medicine in Dane County. Most are inside police stations or city buildings and are open year-round.

What to do:

  • Bring medicines in their original containers.
  • Put pills in a zip-top bag.
  • Keep liquids, creams, and gels in their original container.
  • Place the bag in the drop box.
  • Recycle the empty bottles — remove your name and info first.

✅ Accepted

  • Prescription medicines
  • Over-the-counter medicines
  • Pet medicines
  • Vitamins
  • Inhalers
  • Creams and liquids
  • Patches

❌ Not accepted

  • Needles or syringes
  • EpiPens
  • Oxygen tanks
  • IV bags
  • Medical equipment

Wisconsin also has drug drop boxes outside Dane County. They take the same items as MedDrop, plus vape pens (take the battery out first).

💉 Needles and syringes

⚠️

Never put these in a medicine drop box.

  • Put used needles straight into a hard plastic sharps container — don't bend or break them.
  • No sharps container at home? Ask a nearby pharmacy or clinic where to get one and where to drop off full ones.

🩺 Medical equipment you can't donate

Broken glucose meters, CPAP machines, oxygen tanks, IV bags, and other electronics can't go in the regular trash in Wisconsin.

Dane County Clean Sweep

7020 Maahic Way, Madison

Takes electronics and household chemicals from Dane County residents, year-round.

For Dane County residents and K–12 schools only — not for pharmacies or businesses.

🧱 Bandages and gloves

Used bandages, gauze, and gloves can go in your regular trash, bagged.

If something is soaked in blood, double-bag it.

Key Reminders

  • Don't flush medicines or pour them down the drain.
  • Electronics can't go in regular trash in Wisconsin — take them to Clean Sweep.
  • Hours and accepted items can change — call ahead if you're not sure.
