Medication & Sharps Disposal Guide

Where to safely get rid of expired medications, sharps, and other medical items you can no longer use. Dane County, Wisconsin resources are listed first since most of this is county- and state-specific.

⚠️

Never put sharps (needles, syringes, lancets) in a medication drop box.

Sharps need their own puncture-resistant container and a separate collection site — see the Sharps section below. Mixing sharps into a medication drop box creates real problems and added costs for the police departments that run them.

Looking to donate medication that's still sealed and usable instead of disposing of it? Visit the Drug Repository Site Finder.

Expired or Unwanted Medications

Never flush unused or expired medications down a toilet or sink, and don't just throw them in household trash — flushing can contaminate the local water supply.

Safe Communities Madison-Dane County — MedDrop

Safe Communities runs the local MedDrop program, the primary way to dispose of medication in Dane County. MedDrop boxes are typically located in local police stations or municipal buildings and are available year-round.

  • Example: the UW–Madison Police Department has a MedDrop box in its lobby, available 24/7. Place unused or expired medication in a resealable bag (bags provided on-site) and drop it in the box.
  • Safe Communities also partners with the African American Opioid Coalition and local law enforcement to host National Prescription Drug Take Back Day collection events at multiple Dane County sites each spring and fall.

Search for "Safe Communities Madison-Dane County MedDrop" for a current map of drop box locations.

Permanent Drug Drop Boxes (statewide)

Wisconsin DHS tracks permanent drug drop boxes across the state. These accept prescription and OTC medications, liquids in their original containers, pet medications, and vape pens/e-cigarettes — remove the battery first, since batteries are flammable.

National Prescription Drug Take Back Day

The next Take Back Day is October 24, 2026. These events are staffed, no questions asked, and usually take just a few minutes.

💡

Staff note

Before treating something as disposal, check whether it can be donated back into the Wisconsin Drug Repository Program instead — see the Drug Repository Site Finder. That's a separate pathway, not covered on this page.

Sharps (Needles, Syringes, Lancets)

⚠️

Sharps must never go in a MedDrop or other medication drop box.

This has been a recurring problem in Dane County — sharps placed in medication boxes create added disposal costs for the police departments that run them. Sharps need their own puncture-resistant container and a dedicated collection site.

Where to take sharps

The Wisconsin DNR maintains a map of registered sharps collection stations statewide. Many healthcare providers also accept household sharps even if they aren't formally registered with the DNR — it's worth calling a nearby pharmacy or clinic to ask.

Before you dispose of a sharp

  • Clip the needle point with a needle clipper, or recap/re-sheathe the sharp, to reduce injury risk.
  • Use a puncture-resistant sharps container — not a plastic bag or a soda bottle.

If no collection site is accessible, a clipped (needle removed) syringe can go in household trash as a last resort — but a proper sharps container and a real collection site is always the better option.

Broken or Non-Donatable Equipment

Dead glucose meters, broken CPAP units, and other electronics — it is illegal in Wisconsin to put electronics in regular household trash, since many contain lead, cadmium, and mercury.

Dane County Clean Sweep

7020 Maahic Way, Madison

Accepts electronics and hazardous chemicals from Dane County residents year-round, and performs data-wiping on anything with memory before recycling.

Clean Sweep only accepts electronics from Dane County residents and K–12 schools, not commercial or business sources — this is an individual-patient referral, not a bulk-disposal option for a pharmacy.

Non-Sharp Used Supplies

Soiled bandages, gauze, and PPE are generally fine in regular household trash, bagged. This isn't a regulated hazardous-waste category the way sharps and pharmaceuticals are.

Visibly blood-soaked items are sometimes double-bagged as a courtesy or precaution, but no special disposal program is needed.

Key Reminders

  • Never flush medications or pour them down a drain.
  • Never put sharps in a medication drop box — they need a separate, puncture-resistant container and collection site.
  • Electronics cannot go in regular trash in Wisconsin — take them to Clean Sweep or another registered e-waste site.
  • When in doubt, call ahead — accepted items and hours can change.
