How this screen works
  • Starch is the main concern. Tablets and capsules rarely contain gluten, and other excipients are highly unlikely to contain enough to cause a reaction.
  • Unlabeled starch can come from wheat. In foods, plain "starch" must be corn, but that rule does not apply to medications. Plain starch, pregelatinized starch (usually corn), and sodium starch glycolate (usually potato) can come from any source.
  • A named source settles it. Corn, potato, tapioca, and rice starch are not gluten sources. Wheat starch should be avoided.
  • Sugar alcohols are safe. If a manufacturer says an ingredient is wheat-derived, it is often a sugar alcohol such as mannitol, sorbitol, maltitol, or xylitol, which contains no gluten.
  • Cross-contact in drug plants is extremely unlikely because of the air-quality controls the FDA requires.
  • Olmesartan (Benicar) contains no gluten but can cause a celiac-like enteropathy and is best avoided.

There is no law requiring drug makers to disclose excipient sources. When a starch source is not stated, call the manufacturer and ask what the starch is made from.

Vitamins and supplements: Supplements are not required to disclose gluten, follow gluten-free labeling standards, or be tested for gluten, and many are not listed in DailyMed. Choose supplements labeled gluten-free and read the ingredient list.

Data Limitations: This screen reads inactive ingredient text from labeling submitted to DailyMed. Manufacturers are not required to name excipient sources, and labels can change. A "Unlikely to contain gluten" result means no starch or gluten-grain excipients were listed, not that the product is certified gluten-free.
